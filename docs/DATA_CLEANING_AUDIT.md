## Reviewer's assessment (manual, not auto-generated)

Reviewed all 50 segments and all 10 samples above. Conclusion: **the heuristic
is not over-stripping vehicle-specific product information.** Every one of
the top 50 segments is one of:

- A legal/pricing disclaimer (taxes/fees exclusions, "subject to prior sale",
  recall-notice boilerplate, guarantee terms and conditions).
- A dealer- or franchise-level marketing tagline or "why buy from us"
  paragraph (e.g. `Hertz Car Sales`, `Group 1`), not a fact about the
  specific vehicle.
- Auction-platform mechanics (Buy It Now vs. bidding, "no dealer license
  required") that describe the sales channel, not the car.
- A generic call-to-action ("Contact us today", "Schedule a test drive").

Three segments (#47, #48, #50) are borderline: canned narrative sentences
that describe what a safety feature *does* (e.g. "Pedestrian Impact
Prevention... your vehicle is equipped to better see them and avoid them").
These read closer to product information than pure disclaimer text. However,
the underlying fact - that the vehicle *has* that feature - is preserved
elsewhere: the structured `options`/`optionsCount` columns are untouched by
this pipeline, and the feature name itself typically also appears in the
description's bullet list, separate from the narrative sentence being
stripped. What's removed is the flowery explanation of the feature, not the
fact of its presence.

This is also consistent with the aggregate numbers: average description
length dropped only 1685 -> 1544 characters (8.4%), and only 5.3% of
listings lost more than 40% of their characters. The before/after samples
above show feature bullet lists, condition notes, and vehicle-specific
narrative passing through unchanged; only `<br>` tags are normalised to
spaces and the repeated boilerplate sentences are gone.

**Decision: no change to the heuristic.** Raising the occurrence threshold
or adding a protected-terms list would either leave in more legal
boilerplate (lowering the threshold's effectiveness) or add ongoing
maintenance burden for a problem the data doesn't show evidence of. See
the parent conversation's status report for the full record of this
decision.
